# Sustainability and Community Cleanliness

Practical guidance on reducing waste, plastic pollution, e-waste impacts
and keeping neighbourhoods clean.

## Reducing waste before it is created

The most effective waste management is not creating waste. Buy loose
vegetables instead of pre-packed ones, carry a cloth bag and a steel
bottle, choose refills over new containers, and repair items before
replacing them.

Follow the 5 Rs in order: Refuse what you do not need, Reduce what you
use, Reuse what you can, Recycle what cannot be reused, Rot (compost)
what is organic. A household that refuses and reuses sends far less to
the bin than one that only sorts at the end.

Keywords: waste kam kaise kare, कचरा कैसे घटाएं, zero waste tips,
reduce reuse refuse.

## Plastic pollution explained

Single-use plastics - bags, straws, cutlery, chips wrappers - persist for
hundreds of years, break into microplastics and enter soil, drains,
rivers and the food chain. Clogged drains from plastic bags cause urban
flooding; burned plastic releases toxic fumes.

Practical countermeasures: refuse plastic bags, carry your own, avoid
sachets, sort all clean plastics into the dry stream so they reach
recyclers instead of drains and rivers.

Keywords: plastic pollution kya hai, प्लास्टिक प्रदूषण, plastic bag ban,
microplastics, river plastic.

## E-waste and battery hazards

E-waste is the fastest-growing waste stream. Informal dismantling and
open burning of boards and batteries release lead, mercury and cadmium
into air and groundwater, damaging nerves, kidneys and children's
development.

Citizen actions: hand old electronics to certified recyclers or brand
take-back programmes, never throw household batteries in the dustbin,
never burn batteries, and donate working devices for reuse.

Keywords: e-waste kyun khatarnak, ई-वेस्ट का प्रदूषण, battery burning
danger, hazardous waste awareness.

## Community cleanliness drives

A clean neighbourhood needs both behaviour and infrastructure:
scheduled door-to-door collection, covered community bins, regular
street sweeping, and residents who segregate at source.

Organise a monthly ward or society clean-up drive, nominate a volunteer
to keep the bin area clean, and record recurring dumping spots as
complaints on SmartWaste so the municipal team can act on the pattern
rather than a single pile.

Keywords: swachhata abhiyan, स्वच्छता अभियान, colony safai drive,
neighborhood cleanup, community volunteer.

## Composting and green cover

Composting wet waste cuts methane from landfills and returns nutrients to
soil. Terrace gardens, society greens and avenue trees thrive on home
compost and reduce heat and dust in the lane.

Plant native species, protect existing trees, and avoid dumping waste
around tree bases - plastics around roots kill the tree and contaminate
soil.

Keywords: ped aur compost, पेड़ और खाद, terrace garden waste, green
cover city.

## Responsible disposal of hazardous household items

Paint thinners, batteries, tube lights, medicines, pesticides and
printer cartridges are household hazardous waste. Keep them in a sealed
box, never pour them down the sink or into soil, and use municipal
hazardous-waste collection days or retailer take-back counters.

Mixing chemicals with regular waste poisons the waste pickers who handle
it downstream and contaminates recycling streams.

Keywords: hazardous household waste, रासायनिक कचरा, paint disposal,
tube light disposal, medicine disposal.
