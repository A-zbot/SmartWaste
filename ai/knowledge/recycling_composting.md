# Recycling and Composting Handbook

What can be recycled, how to compost wet waste at home, and how to keep
materials clean enough to actually re-enter the value chain.

## What can and cannot be recycled

Recyclable: paper and newspaper, cardboard, glass bottles and jars, metal
cans and foil, clean plastic bottles and containers (PET, HDPE), old
clothes and textiles, and scrap metal.

Not recyclable in normal streams: used tissues, diapers, sanitary napkins,
greasy food packaging, broken ceramics, thermocol/expanded polystyrene in
most municipalities, and contaminated medical waste.

The rule of thumb: if you can wash it and it was once a container or a
sheet, it is probably recyclable; if it absorbed food, bodily fluids or
chemicals, it is not.

## How to prepare items for recycling

Rinse bottles and cans so no food residue remains - dirty items are
rejected at the material recovery facility. Flatten cardboard and boxes to
save space. Keep labels on; they are removed during processing. Remove
batteries and cables from electronics before handing devices over.

Store clean, dry recyclables separately in a bag or blue bin. Liquids
left in containers ruin entire paper and plastic batches, so empty them
fully.

## Home composting of vegetable waste

Vegetable peels, fruit scraps, tea leaves, coffee grounds, eggshells and
garden trimmings can all be composted. Use a balcony composter, a
terrace pit or a society compost bin.

The recipe is roughly one part green (nitrogen-rich wet scraps) to two
parts brown (carbon-rich dry leaves, shredded cardboard or newspaper).
Keep it moist but not soggy, turn or shake the bin weekly, and ensure
drainage. Finished compost is dark, crumbly and earth-smelling in 4-8
weeks.

Do not compost cooked food, oil, meat, fish or dairy in open balcony
setups - they smell and attract rats and flies. Citrus peels in large
quantities slow decomposition, so add them sparingly.

## Compost bins in societies and apartments

A society composting unit takes the wet waste of the whole building.
Feed it only source-segregated wet waste - no plastics, no packaging, no
sanitary waste. Cover each layer with a handful of dry leaves or shredded
cardboard to control smell and flies.

Assign a rotating volunteer to monitor moisture and pests, keep the bin
lid closed, and use the finished compost in the society garden or
distribute it to residents. Composting cuts the society's waste bill and
the number of collection trips.

## E-waste recycling value and drop points

Gold, copper, aluminium and rare metals inside electronics are recovered
by certified recyclers. Informal dismantling burns plastics and releases
lead and mercury, so always hand devices to a brand take-back scheme, a
registered recycler or a municipal e-waste drive.

Data security matters too: wipe phones and laptops and remove storage
drives before recycling the device.

## Textile, metal and glass recycling

Old clothes in usable condition go to donation bins or textile recyclers;
torn cloth becomes cleaning rags or industrial wiping cloth. Metal scrap -
utensils, wires, window frames - is high value and accepted by every
kabadiwala.

Glass bottles are infinitely recyclable but break easily; wrap them,
keep them separate and return reusable bottles to the vendor where
deposit systems exist.
