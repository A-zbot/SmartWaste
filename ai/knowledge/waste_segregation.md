# Waste Segregation Guide

How to separate household waste into wet (biodegradable) and dry
(recyclable) streams, and where each material belongs. Applies to Indian
municipal systems with green (wet) and blue (dry) bins.

## Wet waste and dry waste separation

Wet waste is anything that rots: kitchen scraps, vegetable peels, fruit
leftovers, cooked food, tea leaves, eggshells and garden trimmings. Keep it
in a separate covered bin, preferably with a drain tray, and hand it to the
green-bin collection or your compost bin.

Dry waste is everything that does not rot: paper, cardboard, plastic
bottles and containers, metal cans, glass, old cloth and thermocol. Rinse
food residue off containers, fold cardboard flat and store dry waste in the
blue bin or a separate bag.

Never mix the two streams. Mixed waste contaminates recyclables, makes wet
waste unfit for composting and increases landfill volume. Separate at the
source - in the kitchen itself - using two small bins rather than sorting
later at the door.

Keywords: गीला सूखा कचरा alag karna, wet dry waste separate kaise,
kachra alag, green bin blue bin, segregation at source.

## Where to throw plastic bottles

Plastic bottles (PET water and soft-drink bottles) are dry recyclable
waste. Empty the bottle, cap it (caps are a different plastic but accepted
by most scrap dealers), rinse if it held liquid and place it in the blue
dry-waste bin or hand it to a kabadiwala / scrap dealer.

Do not burn plastic bottles - burning releases toxic fumes and dioxins.
Do not throw them in wet waste or drains; they choke drains and do not
decompose. Reuse sturdy bottles at home where possible before recycling.

Keywords: plastic bottle kahan feku, प्लास्टिक की बोतल, botal recycling,
PET bottle blue bin.

## Is cardboard recyclable

Yes, cardboard is one of the most reliably recyclable materials. Flatten
boxes, remove tape and staples where easy, keep it dry and place it in the
dry/recyclable bin. Greasy or food-soiled cardboard (pizza boxes, oil
cartons) cannot be recycled - tear off the greasy part and compost the
clean part or dispose it as dry waste.

Wet cardboard loses its fibre value, so keep flattened boxes covered in
monsoon. Small quantities can go to the paper/kabadiwala stream.

Keywords: cardboard recyclable hai kya, कार्डबोर्ड पुनर्चक्रण, carton
recycle, dabba recycling.

## How to separate kitchen and food waste

Scrape food leftovers into a wet-waste bin or composter before washing
utensils. Keep vegetable peels, fruit skins, tea/coffee grounds, eggshells
and bones together as wet waste. Do not put cooked oil, meat bones with
large fat content or dairy in a balcony composter - these attract pests -
put them in the municipal wet-waste stream instead.

Keep a small caddy near the cooking area so separation becomes a habit.
Rinse recyclable packaging before it joins dry waste.

Keywords: kitchen waste alag, खाने का कचरा, food waste segregation,
sabzi ke chhilke.

## Where electronic waste and batteries go

Electronic waste (e-waste) - phones, laptops, chargers, TVs, fridges,
circuit boards - must never go into household garbage. It contains lead,
mercury and other hazardous materials. Hand e-waste to a registered
e-waste recycler, a brand take-back scheme, a manufacturer dealer, or a
municipal e-waste collection drive.

Batteries (AA/AAA cells, phone batteries, power-bank cells, inverter
batteries) are hazardous dry waste. Do not throw them in the blue bin with
regular recyclables - lithium and lead cells can cause fires and soil
contamination. Use battery collection boxes at electronics shops, municipal
hazardous-waste counters or the brand's take-back programme. Never burn
batteries and never puncture them.

Keywords: e-waste kahan de, बैटरी कहाँ फेंकें, electronic waste disposal,
battery disposal box, mobile phone recycle.

## Medical and sanitary waste at home

Home healthcare waste - used bandages, medicine strips, dressing cotton,
diapers and sanitary napkins - must be double-bagged and kept separate from
both wet and dry household waste. Do not recycle medicine strips or
syringes. Dispose through the municipal biomedical waste route or a clinic
that accepts sharps.

Used masks and gloves from illness should be bagged separately and
handled as biomedical waste, not thrown loose on the street.

Keywords: medical waste ghar par, चिकित्सा कचरा, sanitary napkin disposal,
diaper kahan feku, biomedical waste.

## Construction debris and drain waste

Construction debris - concrete chunks, bricks, tiles, plaster, wood scraps
- is not household waste. Arrange a licensed debris remover or use the
municipal construction-debris collection point; dumping it on roads or in
drains is illegal and blocks storm water.

Never pour cooking oil, paint, cement slurry or chemicals into drains.
Grease solidifies and blocks sewer lines. Solid waste in gutters causes
waterlogging and mosquito breeding - keep street drains free of plastic
and silt.

Keywords: malba kahan de, निर्माण मलबा, construction debris disposal,
drain blockage kachra, nali saaf.

## Bin colours and collection norms

In most Indian municipal systems: green bin or green bag = wet/biodegradable
waste; blue bin = dry recyclable waste; red or black biomedical bag =
hazardous/medical waste. Some cities add a third yellow stream for
sanitary waste.

Segregate every day before the collection vehicle arrives, do not overfill
bins, and keep the area around community bins clean. Mixed loads are often
rejected by processing plants, so correct segregation at home is what makes
recycling actually work.

Keywords: green bin blue bin kya hota hai, कूड़ेदान रंग, dustbin colours,
doorstep collection.
